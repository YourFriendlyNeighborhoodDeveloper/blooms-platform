# Before taking real customers

Every item must be done before the platform takes a real order. The dashboard's **Launch checklist**
checks most of them automatically.

## The agreement

- [ ] A signed agreement between the business and the developer, covering ownership, hosting, support and costs
- [ ] The owner's written approval to launch

## The business's own accounts

- [ ] Stripe, in the business's name, verified, with payouts set up
- [ ] An email sending service, with the business's domain verified
- [ ] Hosting, and the domain name, owned or controlled as agreed
- [ ] Twilio and carrier registration, only if text messages are wanted

## Setup, entered and approved in the dashboard

- [ ] Opening hours and closures
- [ ] The team, roles, schedules and crews
- [ ] Real products with photos. The demonstration products removed.
- [ ] Delivery zones, fees and times
- [ ] Ordering rules, approved by the owner
- [ ] Delivery rules, approved by the owner
- [ ] Sales tax, confirmed by the accountant and approved by the owner
- [ ] Refund policy, approved by the owner
- [ ] Wedding services, rentals, deposit and cancellation policies
- [ ] Privacy policy and terms, from the lawyer
- [ ] Customer message wording reviewed

## Verified in the real environment

- [ ] A test payment, a declined payment, and a refund, in Stripe's test mode
- [ ] Test emails received in real inboxes, including Gmail and Outlook, and not in spam
- [ ] One full rehearsal: an order, a delivery, a wedding inquiry, a quote, a deposit and a refund
- [ ] Backups running, copied off the server, and a restore tested
- [ ] Monitoring and alerts reaching someone who will act on them
- [ ] Staff trained

## Going live

- [ ] Live Stripe keys connected, and live payments deliberately turned on
- [ ] The concept notices removed, and search engines allowed, with the owner's approval
- [ ] One small real order placed and refunded, to confirm everything end to end

Until every box is ticked, the platform keeps its safeguards: simulated payments, captured messages,
manual review of orders, and the concept notices.
