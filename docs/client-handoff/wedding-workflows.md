# Weddings and events: from inquiry to confirmed project

## 1. The customer plans, at any hour

On the website, **Plan your event** takes seven steps:

1. **The event:** names, date, times, venue, guest count, budget, and how they would like to meet.
2. **Their style:** colors, theme, floral style, what they are picturing, must-haves, and inspiration photos. Photos are stored privately, and only the team can see them.
3. **Flowers and rentals:** they choose services and rental items, with quantities.
4. **The estimate:** published prices, estimate ranges, and items that need a custom quote, kept clearly apart, with estimated tax and deposit. It says plainly that it is a starting point, not a final price.
5. **The date check:** crews, people, rentals, the shop's event limit for that day, closures and notice. If the date does not work, it says why and suggests nearby dates. They can still send the plan for the team to review.
6. **Send.** Everything is saved, the customer gets an acknowledgment, and the team is alerted.
7. **Consultation:** they choose from times when someone who takes consultations is actually working. The time is reserved, and two customers can never book the same slot.

An inquiry never reserves a date by itself. Nothing is promised until the team says so.

## 2. The team reviews

The inquiry appears on the **Pipeline** board straight away, with everything the customer sent.
From there, the team can:

- **Accept and start planning**, **ask a question**, **suggest another date**, or **decline**.
- **Hold the date** for a few days while talking, which reserves crews and rentals until the hold ends.
- **Book a consultation** for the customer.
- **Build a quote** from the selections, adjust prices and quantities, set the deposit, and **send it**.

Every action is recorded, and the customer's private page shows the current status.

## 3. The customer accepts and pays the deposit

The customer opens the quote from their email, reviews it, types their name to accept, and pays the
deposit online. Accepting holds the date. Paying the deposit, and a signed contract if you require
one, confirms it.

## 4. A confirmed project

On confirmation, the system creates the project:

- crews and rentals reserved for setup and teardown, so they cannot be double-booked,
- a payment schedule with the balance due date,
- staff tasks with due dates, such as ordering flowers, confirming the timeline, final counts, preparation, setup and teardown,
- calendar entries for the event, setup and teardown,
- material requirements for purchasing,
- reminders to the customer and the team as milestones approach.

The customer's project page shows the timeline, payments and anything still owed.

## What decides whether a date is available

| Limit | Set under |
| --- | --- |
| Most events in a day | Settings → Weddings & events |
| Event crews, and people per crew | Team → Event crews, and Settings → Weddings & events |
| Who works that day | Team → each person's schedule and days off |
| Rental quantities, plus cleaning time after each return | Rentals |
| Setup, teardown, travel and break times | Settings → Weddings & events |
| Minimum notice, and preparation days | Settings → Weddings & events |
| Closures | Settings → Hours & closures |

When a date is full, the system names each limit that is reached, so you know whether to add a crew,
schedule another person, or say no.

## Pipeline stages

New inquiry, Consultation scheduled, Planning, Quote sent, Awaiting deposit, Confirmed,
In preparation, Event scheduled, Completed and Closed. Rename them, or add your own, with
**Manage stages**.
